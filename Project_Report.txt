EXPLORING THE RELATIONSHIP BETWEEN NITRATE AND CANCER IN WISCONSIN
An Interactive Geospatial Analysis Tool

THE PROBLEM

Is there a connection between nitrate levels in Wisconsin's groundwater and cancer rates? This question matters because many rural communities rely on private wells that can be contaminated by agricultural runoff and fertilizers. Understanding this relationship could help protect public health and guide environmental policy.

The challenge is that we have point data (nitrate measurements from specific wells) and need to compare it with area data (cancer rates for entire census tracts). Traditional analysis methods don't handle this spatial mismatch well, and existing tools lack the flexibility to explore different scenarios interactively.

THE SOLUTION

I built an interactive web application that lets users explore the nitrate-cancer relationship in real time. The app uses Flask (Python) for the analytical backend and Leaflet.js for the interactive map interface.

Key Features:
- Inverse Distance Weighting (IDW) to estimate nitrate levels between measurement points
- Two analysis modes: traditional grid interpolation and innovative hexagonal tessellation
- Adjustable parameters (distance decay, hexagon size) with immediate visual feedback
- Statistical analysis with correlation and regression results
- Three color-coded legends for nitrate levels, cancer rates, and model residuals

Technical Innovations:
The hexagonal approach was particularly challenging but rewarding. Unlike rectangular grids, hexagons provide more uniform spatial coverage and look better on maps. I had to solve the problem of perfectly fitting hexagons to the study area with no gaps or overlaps, then clip them to census tract boundaries to ensure we only analyze areas with valid cancer data.

The app calculates area-weighted averages when hexagons cross multiple census tracts, ensuring statistical validity. It also shows regression residuals—areas where the model over- or under-predicts cancer rates—which can reveal spatial patterns that deserve further investigation.

WHAT WE FOUND

The analysis consistently shows a moderate positive correlation between nitrate concentrations and cancer rates across Wisconsin. The relationship isn't perfect (R-squared values around 0.15-0.35), which means nitrate explains about 15-35% of the variation in cancer rates.

Spatial Patterns:
- Higher nitrate levels cluster in agricultural regions, especially central and southwestern Wisconsin
- Cancer rates show a more complex pattern, with some high-cancer areas coinciding with high nitrate levels
- The correlation is consistent across different analysis parameters, suggesting it's not just a statistical artifact

The residuals analysis reveals interesting spatial patterns. Some areas consistently have higher cancer rates than the model predicts, suggesting additional risk factors beyond nitrate. Other areas have lower rates than expected, possibly indicating protective factors or reduced exposure to other carcinogens.

IMPLICATIONS

This study provides evidence for a measurable but moderate association between groundwater nitrate and cancer in Wisconsin. While the relationship is real and statistically significant, nitrate alone doesn't explain most cancer variation—other factors clearly matter too.

For public health, this supports continued monitoring of nitrate levels, especially in agricultural areas. However, it also emphasizes that cancer prevention requires a comprehensive approach considering multiple environmental and lifestyle factors.

Methodologically, the project demonstrates the value of interactive analysis tools. Being able to adjust parameters and immediately see results enables much deeper exploration than traditional static analyses. The hexagonal tessellation approach offers advantages over conventional grid methods and could be applied to other spatial analysis problems.

FUTURE DIRECTIONS

Future research should incorporate additional variables like other contaminants, air quality, socioeconomic factors, and lifestyle variables to build more complete models of cancer risk. Analyzing changes over time would also strengthen our understanding of causal relationships.

The interactive analysis approach developed here could be adapted for other environmental health questions, providing researchers and policymakers with flexible tools for exploring complex spatial relationships in real time.

CONCLUSION

This project successfully developed an innovative tool for exploring environmental health relationships while providing evidence for a moderate but consistent association between groundwater nitrate and cancer rates in Wisconsin. The findings support continued environmental monitoring while highlighting the complex, multifactorial nature of cancer causation.
